01 / THE QUESTION

“Could I actually
build this idea?”

I’ve been tinkering with AI and Codex, trying small ideas and seeing how far they can go. With a MacBook camera and an external webcam on my desk, I wondered whether one could watch for gestures while Zoom kept using the other.

There wasn’t a specific business problem to solve. I wanted to explore Apple’s video and hand-pose capabilities, use Swift to connect the pieces, and see whether the result could become a usable little application.

Vela is that experiment: real source code, a native interface, and plenty learned from the things that needed another attempt.

02 / UNDER THE HOOD

Swift connects the pieces.
Apple provides the building blocks.

The recognition comes from Apple’s Vision framework, rather than the Swift language itself. Vela adds the gesture rules and the behaviour around them.

01

Capture

AVFoundation selects the built-in camera and delivers video frames. A mirrored preview makes it easy to position your hand.

AVCaptureSession
02

Estimate the pose

Vision estimates hand-joint positions and confidence values. No custom hand model was trained for this project.

VNDetectHumanHandPoseRequest
03

Recognise intent

Swift rules interpret the landmarks. Hold timing, smoothing and a release-to-reset latch help distinguish a deliberate gesture from movement.

GestureCore
04

Control the app

macOS Accessibility reads Zoom’s controls. Vela uses native controls or menu shortcuts, then checks the resulting state.

ApplicationServices
⌘

A native Mac application. SwiftUI builds the interface. AppKit handles the window, Dock and menu bar. Codable and UserDefaults keep editable mappings on your Mac. Codex helped build and iterate on the app; it is not a runtime dependency.

04 / THE INTERESTING PART

The learning was
in the iteration.

Recognising hand landmarks was only the start. A natural thumb position could reject an open palm. A hand resting on the desk could keep the reset latched. A mode selector plus a separate switch made “Live” confusing.

Those details shaped the implementation: more tolerant palm rules, a visible active area, and one clear Paused / Practice / Live selector. macOS signing and Accessibility approvals needed attention too.

This is an experimental reference project, not a production-ready business solution or an official Zoom integration. Extending it to another application means building and testing that application’s control path.

Release notes & SHA-256 checksum ↗

Local processing. Vela does not upload or save camera frames and does not capture microphone audio. Meeting recordings are made by Zoom.

First launch

  1. Open the DMG and drag Vela into Applications.
  2. Open Vela from Applications. If blocked as an unidentified developer, and you trust this release, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.
  3. Authenticate if requested, then confirm Open.
  4. Allow Camera access and enable Vela’s Accessibility permission. Begin in Practice mode.

This build is ad-hoc signed, not Developer ID signed or Apple-notarised. Use only the app-specific exception; do not disable Gatekeeper globally. Stop if macOS reports malware or a damaged app. Managed Macs may restrict exceptions.
